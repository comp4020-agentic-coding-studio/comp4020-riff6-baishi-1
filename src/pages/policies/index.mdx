## Late work and extensions

Work handed in late loses 5% of the available mark per calendar day, to seven days,
after which it is marked as submitted with a zero recorded and feedback given
anyway — a late forgery is still worth critiquing. Extensions are granted before
the deadline, not after; email the convenor and say what you need, rather than
waiting to see if you need it.

## Materials and studio safety

The studios handle solvents, pigments and small edge tools. You'll be shown the
safe handling of anything new before you use it, and the workshop keeps a
materials data sheet for everything on the bench. Anyone working with an
unfamiliar material for the first time without a demonstration should stop and
ask, not proceed on the assumption it's like something they've used before —
most studio injuries in this course's short history have come from exactly that
assumption.

## Academic integrity, and the thing this course makes weird

Most courses ask you not to submit someone else's work as your own. This one
asks you to submit a *convincing copy* of someone else's technique as your own
craft demonstration — that's [Assignment 1](/assessments/the-convincing-copy/)
by design. The line
is authorship of the object, not resemblance to a source: a forgery you made,
in your own hand, of a style you studied and declared, is the assignment. A
forgery you did not make — an object, a text or a generated artefact you are
presenting as your own labour when it isn't — is the thing integrity policy
still means. Every practical submission carries a short materials-and-method
note saying what you made it with and what you copied it from; that note is
where the declaring happens, and a submission without one is treated as
undeclared regardless of how good the work is.

The same distinction covers generative AI: using a model to draft prose you
then rewrite in your own argument is normal coursework, declared like any other
source. Presenting a model's output as your own critical judgement — the essay
that reads someone else's forgery and says why it works — is not, because the
judgement is the assessable skill, and it's the one thing in this course
nobody else can do the noticing for you.

## Getting help

The convenor and tutor are named on the [people page](/people/), with contact
preferences for each. A studio question is almost always better asked in the
studio than by email, since half of what a tutor answers is legible only with
the material in your hands.
