Nothing on this page is compulsory. The studio is stocked well enough that you
can pass every assessment on bench supplies alone, and every year somebody
does. The list exists because most people find the work goes better with a
small kit of their own — a brush that behaves the way you expect matters more
in this course than in most, since half of what you are copying is somebody
else's hand and the other half is your own getting out of the way.

Buy nothing before Studio 1. You'll have handled the bench stock by the end of
week 1 and will know which three things you actually want to own.

## What the studio supplies

Free, on the bench, every session: pigments and mediums, solvents and their
extraction, sized panels and offcut board, laid and wove paper stock, the
ageing cupboard, the hot plate, the press, all edge tools and the loupes. The
workshop keeps a materials data sheet for everything listed here — see the
[safety section of the policies page](/policies/#materials-and-studio-safety)
before you handle anything unfamiliar.

You do not need to buy any solvent. Take nothing solvent-based home.

## Paint — weeks 2 to 6

| Item | Why | Rough cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Three sable or synthetic-sable rounds, sizes 0, 2 and 6 | The bench brushes are shared and therefore blunt; a signature is stroke rhythm and a worn tip flattens yours | $40–70 |
| A palette knife with some spring in it | Ground layers and impasto; the stiff studio ones fight you | $12–20 |
| A sketchbook you are willing to ruin | Every copy starts as twenty bad tracings | $15–25 |

## Prose — weeks 3 and 7

| Item | Why | Rough cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A dip pen holder and a few nibs (one fine, one flexible) | Hand imitation needs a nib that responds to pressure the way the original writer's did | $20–35 |
| Iron gall or walnut ink, 30 ml | Period-plausible and it ages honestly; the studio's india ink does not | $12–18 |
| Blotting paper | | $5 |

## Currency — week 8

| Item | Why | Rough cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A 10× loupe of your own | You'll want it at home, in shops, and for the rest of your life | $25–60 |
| Fine-point burnisher or an agate | Intaglio-style work by hand | $18–30 |

Engraving plates, the press and the security-pattern stock are studio-only and
stay in the building.

## Code — weeks 11 and 12

Nothing to buy. Bring a laptop you can install git on and run a local shell in;
if you can't, say so in week 1 and the studio will lend you one for the block.

## The honest total

A comfortable personal kit lands around **$150–200** across the semester,
spread over three purchases rather than one. A minimal kit — three brushes, a
nib, a loupe — is closer to **$80**. Zero is genuinely workable.

If cost is a barrier, email the convenor before week 3 rather than quietly
going without; the school holds a small materials fund for exactly this and it
is easier to draw on early than in week 9.

## A note on what you buy versus what you make

Several weeks ask you to age a surface, mix a ground or size a panel yourself
when a shop-bought equivalent exists. That is deliberate and it is assessed:
the [materials-and-method note](/policies/#academic-integrity-and-the-thing-this-course-makes-weird)
attached to every practical submission has to say what you made and what you
bought, and a copy whose convincingness came out of a bottle is a different
claim from one that came out of your hands. Both are allowed. Only one of them
is undeclared, and that is the one that loses marks.
